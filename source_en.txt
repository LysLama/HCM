B. CONTENT
I. HO CHI MINH THOUGHT ON GREAT NATIONAL UNITY
1. The Role of Great National Unity
a) Great national unity is a strategic issue that determines the success of the revolution

In Ho Chi Minh’s thought, great national unity is a long-term and consistent strategy of the Vietnamese revolution. He clearly pointed out:
“History teaches us this lesson: When our people are united as one, our country will gain independence and freedom. Conversely, when our people are not united, we will be invaded by foreign powers.”

Great national unity is a matter of survival for the Vietnamese nation; therefore, this strategy has been maintained throughout both the national democratic revolution and the socialist revolution. In each revolutionary stage, faced with different requirements and tasks, policies and methods of mobilizing great unity may and must be adjusted to suit different groups. However, the policy of great national unity must never be changed, because it is the decisive factor in the success or failure of the revolution.

From practical experience in building the great national unity bloc, Ho Chi Minh summarized many principles of a truth-like nature regarding its role and strength:
“Unity is our strength,”
“Unity is an invincible force enabling us to overcome difficulties and achieve victory,”
“Unity is strength; unity is victory,”
“Unity is strength and the key to success,”
“There is now one very important point, which is also the fundamental point. If this point is well implemented, it will give rise to many good outcomes. That point is unity.”

He concluded:

“Unity, unity, great unity
Success, success, great success.”

b) Great national unity is a primary objective and task of the Vietnamese revolution

For Ho Chi Minh, great national unity is not only a strategic slogan but also a long-term objective of the revolution. As the Communist Party is the leading force of the Vietnamese revolution, great national unity must necessarily be identified as the Party’s foremost task. This task must be thoroughly implemented in all areas, from guidelines, viewpoints, and policies to the Party’s practical activities.

In his closing speech at the founding ceremony of the Vietnam Workers’ Party on March 3, 1951, Ho Chi Minh declared:
“The purpose of the Vietnam Workers’ Party can be summed up in eight words: UNITY OF THE ENTIRE PEOPLE, SERVICE TO THE FATHERLAND.”

The revolution is the cause of the masses, carried out by the masses and for the masses. Great national unity is an objective requirement of the revolutionary cause and an objective demand of the people in their struggle for self-liberation. Without unity, the people themselves would fail in the struggle for their own interests.

Clearly aware of this, the Communist Party must carry out its mission to awaken, unite, and guide the masses, transforming their objective and spontaneous demands into conscious demands and organized reality within the great unity bloc. This creates a combined strength in the struggle for national independence, freedom for the people, and human happiness.

2. The Forces of the Great National Unity Bloc
a) The subjects of the great national unity bloc

According to Ho Chi Minh, the subjects of the great national unity bloc include the entire people—all Vietnamese who love their country—from all social classes, strata, sectors, genders, age groups, ethnicities, religious communities, political parties, and so on.

In Ho Chi Minh’s thought, “the people” refers both to specific Vietnamese individuals and to the broad masses as a collective. Both are subjects of the great national unity bloc. Great national unity means uniting and gathering all people into a single unified bloc, without discrimination based on ethnicity, class, social status, political affiliation, religion, age, gender, occupation, or place of residence—whether inside or outside the country—toward a common goal:

“Anyone with talent, virtue, ability, and a sincere desire to serve the Fatherland and the people, we unite with them.”

Here, “we” refers to the subject—both the Communist Party of Vietnam in particular and the Vietnamese people as a whole.

Ho Chi Minh further emphasized that in building the great national unity bloc, it is essential to firmly uphold the working-class standpoint and harmoniously resolve relationships between classes and the nation in order to mobilize all forces. No force should be excluded as long as it is loyal and willing to serve the Fatherland and does not betray the interests of the people.

His thought provided orientation for building the great national unity bloc throughout the entire course of the Vietnamese revolution, from the national democratic revolution to the socialist revolution.

b) The foundation of the great national unity bloc

To build the great national unity bloc, it is necessary to clearly identify its foundation and the forces that constitute that foundation. Ho Chi Minh stated:

“Great unity means first and foremost uniting the vast majority of the people, and the vast majority of the people are workers, peasants, and other working people. This is the foundation of great unity. It is like the foundation of a house or the roots of a tree. But once there is a solid foundation and strong roots, it is still necessary to unite other social strata.”

Thus, according to Ho Chi Minh, the foundational forces of the great national unity bloc are the working class, the peasantry, and the intelligentsia. The stronger and more consolidated this foundation becomes, the broader the great national unity bloc can expand, and no force will be able to weaken it.

Within the great national unity bloc, special attention must be paid to the “core” factor—unity and solidarity within the Party—because this is the condition for unity in society at large. The stronger the Party’s unity, the stronger national unity becomes. Party unity, national unity, and the close bond between the Party and the people have created the internal strength of the Vietnamese revolution, enabling it to overcome all difficulties and challenges, defeat all enemies, and advance toward the final victory of the revolution.
